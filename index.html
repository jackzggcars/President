Pick a party, inherit its map

Every state starts where it actually voted in the 2024 election, relative to the nation. California and New York are Democratic fortresses, Texas and Wyoming are Republican, and the race is decided in a handful of states that swing on a few points.

Democratic

Maine and Nebraska split their votes

Each awards two electoral votes statewide and one per congressional district, so Nebraska's 2nd and Maine's 2nd are battlegrounds of their own.

Real turnout, real popular vote

Each state's vote total is based on its actual 2024 turnout, so winning California by a mile can still lose you the Electoral College.

Spoilers and the debate threshold

Third-party candidates draw votes from the side closest to them, and they need 15% nationally to qualify for the debate.

Voter groups and demographics

Each state carries its own mix of Black, Hispanic, rural, college-educated and older voters, so winning over a group moves the states where it matters most.

Polls can miss

Every pollster has a margin of error and some have a house lean. On election night the actual result can land a few points away from the average.

Recounts and contingent elections

States decided by under half a point go to a recount, and if no one reaches 270, the House of Representatives picks the president.

Win, then govern

Election night is only the beginning. Take the oath and run the country month by month, from your first week in office to midterms, a re-election fight and, if you make it, a second term.

Congress counts every vote

Bills need 218 in the House and, unless you use budget reconciliation or end the filibuster, 60 in the Senate.

Executive orders meet the Supreme Court

Act alone and your orders can be challenged. The Court's makeup decides whether they survive, and one vacancy could change it.

Act whenever you want

Beyond the monthly crisis, push your own agenda any time: bills, executive orders and addresses, with staff ideas and full reactions.

Twelve economic indicators

Growth, inflation, jobs, gas prices, the border, crime, the debt and more move every month and drive your approval.

A cabinet that actually works

Eight Senate-confirmed secretaries who lead your policies, advise you in a crisis, use signature powers, campaign in the midterms and face oversight hearings.

A real calendar of events

Press conferences, Supreme Court decision days, shutdown fights, hurricanes, foreign crises, a midterm Senate battleground, a primary challenge, the convention, a re-election debate and an October surprise.

Lobbyists and donors

Industry PACs and lobbyists offer money and political capital in exchange for favors. Take the deal and it may become a scandal months later.

A real re-election campaign

Five months on the trail as an incumbent, with a convention, a debate and an October surprise, then election night. Win, and serve until 2037. Historians rank you at the end.
